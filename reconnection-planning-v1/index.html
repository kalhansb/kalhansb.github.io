How one robot explores

Each robot plans on a flat map of 0.2 m squares: not seen yet, free, or blocked. Blocked means a trunk plus a 1.5 m safety ring.     Splitting the board: everyone solves the same problem

    The area is a board of 10 × 10 squares, 10 m each. Each robot keeps its own copy of the board and shares it in its beacon. Every robot shares out the unfinished squares as one tour per robot, so that the last robot finishes as early as possible: it inserts squares greedily, then tidies each tour. Robots with the same board and the same positions get the same answer without talking, and each drives only its own tour. In this toy, a robot covers a square by reaching its middle.

      B

      What every arm shares

      The arms add no new way of choosing goals. They decide when a robot stops exploring to drive somewhere else. The pieces they decide with are the same in every arm.

      Scripted toy

      Hearing each other and swapping maps

      Every robot sends a beacon once a second, in every arm. It carries the robot's position and goal, which robots it can hear, what it is doing, whether it has finished, how much map it has sent and received, its board and its tour. In the sim, whether a beacon gets through depends on how many trunks are in the way.

      0 s
      • ·Present: a beacon heard directly in the last 10 s. Beacons passed on by another robot don't count.
      • ·In contact: I hear you, and your beacon says you hear me.
      • ·Exchanged: each side has received everything the other had sent when contact began. Gives up after 120 s with no progress, or 600 s in total.

        What the robot does next

        A robot stores its commitments (a booked meeting, a chase under way) but not its current activity. Every tick it takes the first line below that holds. Flip the conditions to see what each arm picks. The arms differ only in which lines exist.

        Explore is everything in Part A. Every other activity is a plain drive to a point. It arrives within 1.5 m. If it moves less than 0.2 m in 15 s, it makes a short escape move of 1.5–6 m instead of black-listing anything. A proximity guard runs on every drive, except when both robots are meeting. "Whole team finished" means every robot has been heard directly saying it finished, or any beacon says the team finished. It is never cleared.

        pursuit: go and find a teammate when it pays

        R1 loses R2 and keeps exploring. Once R2 has been silent for 90 s, R1 runs a value check every 10 s. When chasing finally pays, R1 drives to where it predicts R2 will be, then to R2's last goal. When it hears R2, it follows at 10 m until the maps are swapped. Only R1's checks are shown. R2 runs the same rules, and in this toy its value check keeps saying no.

        0 s
        R1's chase check

            Where a chase goes

            • First, the predicted spot on the tour the teammate last broadcast.
            • Then its last goal, then its last heard position.
            • If all three come up empty, the chase has failed. A chase has a 600 s limit.

            The value check

            • R1 must know a square that R2 doesn't.
            • "Ignore R2" is when R1 finishes doing R2's squares too. "Chase" is the drive to R2 plus the shared finish.
            • R1 goes only if chasing finishes strictly sooner. If the solver refuses, the check says yes.

            After a chase

            • The chase ends when the maps are swapped, the chase fails, it hits the limit, or something higher on the list takes over.
            • A new chase waits at least 90 s.
            Scripted toy · v1 rules

            rendezvous: agree a time and place, then keep it

            While the robots are still together at the start, robot 0 (R1) proposes a meeting square, with meetings every 300 s. Each robot books the next meeting everyone can reach. Each works out when to set off from a straight-line estimate, and drives to its own spot on a 3 m ring. The meeting counts once every pair has swapped maps; R1 then picks a fresh square. In the second meeting R3 gets stuck and misses it.

            0 s

              Booking and setting off

              • A robot books a meeting once the team has not been all in contact for 15 s.
              • It sets off when straight-line distance ÷ 0.40 m/s × 1.2 would get it there just in time. A finished robot sets off at once.
              • If everyone meets by chance before setting off, the booking is cancelled.

              Ending a meeting

              • Met: every pair swapped maps. R1 then issues the next plan version with a fresh square.
              • Patience: 120 s with no progress after arriving. Window: 120 s after the meeting time, unless maps are still being swapped. Backstop: 600 s.
              • If a pair loses contact on the way, the higher-numbered robot drives to the other's last position.

              Plans and finished robots

              • Any robot adopts a newer plan version from any beacon, relayed or not. If robots hold different versions, they alternate between two squares until they agree again.
              • A finished robot keeps attending meetings (Wait). It drives home once the team has finished or it has missed 2 meetings in a row.
              • If the team was never all in contact, there is no plan, and the arm behaves like off.
              Scripted toy · v1 rules

              hybrid: both, and the meeting comes first

              Both switches are on. A chase may start only if no booking has set off and the next departure is more than 120 s away. Meet sits above Chase on the list, so once the departure time comes, the meeting takes over. Chasing away from the meeting square makes the trip back longer, which brings the departure time forward.
